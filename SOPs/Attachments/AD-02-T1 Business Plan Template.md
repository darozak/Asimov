## 3. The Problem and Opportunity

*The policy and societal gap around beneficial human–machine intelligence interaction.
Why now. What happens absent this work. Supporting evidence and sources.*

## 4. Programs and Activities

*Each program: what it is, who it serves, the activities, and the outputs. Typical
areas: research and policy analysis, model legislation drafting, coalition building,
public education, legislator engagement.*

## 5. Policy and Legislative Strategy

*Target jurisdictions and legislative bodies, priority policy objectives, the pathway
from draft to enacted legislation, allies and opposition, and the advocacy approach
(within nonprofit lobbying limits — see Compliance).*

## 6. Stakeholders and Ecosystem

*Legislators and staff, agencies, allied nonprofits and coalitions, academic and
industry partners, funders, the public. For each: their interest, and how the
organization engages them.*

## 7. Organization and Governance

*Legal form and tax status, board structure, staffing and roles (current and
planned), advisors, decision rights, and how the AI and the User collaborate to run
the organization.*

## 8. Operations

*Where work happens, the tools and systems (this repository and its SOPs among them),
recurring operating rhythms, and key partners or vendors.*

## 9. Funding Model and Development

*Revenue sources (grants, individual donors, major gifts, earned revenue),
fundraising strategy and pipeline, and the plan to diversify and sustain funding.*

## 10. Financial Plan

*Current-year budget summary, multi-year projection, reserve policy, and key
financial assumptions. Detailed figures may live in an AD-02 attachment; link it
here.*

## 11. Roadmap and Milestones

*Time-phased goals (e.g. next quarter, next year, three years) with concrete,
verifiable milestones.*

## 12. Risks and Mitigations

*Table: risk, likelihood, impact, mitigation, owner. Include legal/compliance,
funding, political, reputational, and key-person risks.*

## 13. Compliance and Legal

*Nonprofit status obligations, lobbying activity limits and tracking, conflict-of-
interest policy, required filings, and their owners and cadences.*

## 14. Metrics and Evaluation

*The indicators used to judge progress — outputs and outcomes — how each is measured,
the data source, the cadence, and current baseline/target.*
